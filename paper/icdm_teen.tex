\begin{abstract}
Step caching accelerates diffusion transformers by reusing a block residual
across neighbouring solver steps. The resulting speedup is training-free, but
the image degrades. A learned corrector can predict that reuse error; the
standard way to train it is residual regression. We show that this objective is
misaligned with deployed image quality, and we replace it with
\ourmethod{} (\emph{pathwise adjoint correction}): the same architecture
trained against latent-trajectory deviation by reverse-mode differentiation
through the cached rollout. The change is strictly stronger at inference and
costs about twenty minutes of training.

Cache error is integrated along the sampling trajectory: an anchor refreshes
the cache, not the latent, so a per-step residual target is defined on a
trajectory the deployed corrector never visits. \ourmethod{} improves image
quality at every injection granularity we tested
($K{=}1$ to $28$, all significant, $t{=}2.7$ to $7.0$, $n{=}72$).
At a fixed step count on PixArt-$\Sigma$-XL-2-512, \ourmethod{} reaches
SSIM $0.6820$ against the exact output at $3.41\times$
speedup. It outperforms a reproduction of TaylorSeer's extrapolation mechanism
by $+0.0946$ SSIM (\mbox{$t{=}14.5$}, $72$ of $72$ paired cases) at an $8.4\%$
speedup cost, and a reproduction of Block Caching by $+0.0490$ ($t{=}8.4$)
while running $12\%$ faster. Under ImageReward the separation is larger and one
verdict reverses: both reproduced mechanisms score \emph{below} verbatim
caching, while learned correction is the only method that improves on it.
The measurement that motivated the change is equally sharp: driving residual
accuracy $25$--$43\%$ better by matched-data retraining buys only $+0.005$ to
$+0.013$ SSIM, so residual error is a poor guide to what a corrector is worth.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sampling a diffusion transformer \cite{peebles2023dit} costs one full stack
forward pass per solver step. Step caching exploits the smoothness of that
trajectory: an
\emph{anchor} step stores each block's residual $R=\mathrm{block}(h)-h$, and
the next $i-1$ \emph{reuse} steps skip the block and add the stored residual
instead \cite{ma2024deepcache,selvaraju2024fora,chen2024deltadit}. The method
is training-free and fast, and it visibly degrades the image.

Two families of remedies exist. Training-free extrapolation forecasts residual
drift from its own history \cite{liu2024teacache,liu2025taylorseer}. Learned
correction trains a small network \cite{wimbauer2024blockcaching,ma2024l2c} to
predict the reuse error
\begin{equation}
\dR \;=\; R_{\text{true}} - R_{\text{anchor}},
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
which is exactly the error verbatim reuse commits. This paper studies the
second family. Its default training signal is supervised regression on
\eqref{eq:target}, and every subsequent design choice --- architecture,
checkpoint selection, and how much correction to inject --- is then made in
residual space.

We show that this signal is misaligned with deployed quality, and that a
cheap replacement exists. Cache error is integrated along the sampling
trajectory, while residual error is pointwise; pushing residual accuracy
harder therefore buys almost nothing in image quality. We keep the
architecture, the cache schedule and the deployment rule, and train the same
corrector against the deviation of the latent trajectory by differentiating
through the cached rollout. Because a cached block never executes, that graph
is cheap. The resulting method, \ourmethod{}, improves image quality at every
injection granularity we tested, and at a fixed step count it outperforms
reproductions of two published mechanisms on three fidelity metrics and on a
preference model.

Concretely, we contribute:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A diagnosis of the residual objective.} The cached trajectory's
deviation from the exact one does not reset at cache refreshes: it climbs
almost monotonically ($0.0089$ at step~1 to $0.6092$ at step~19). An anchor
refreshes the cache, not the latent, so error already injected into the state
is permanent. Residual error is pointwise; trajectory error integrates.
\item \textbf{Matched-data residual retraining.} Matching
training-data volume across granularities and training every rung to
convergence improves residual error by $25$--$43\%$ and image SSIM by only
$+0.005$ to $+0.013$, against $+0.033$ for the decision to add correction at
all.
\item \textbf{\ourmethod{}.} We differentiate through the cached
rollout. A cached block never
runs, so the graph over one reuse step is $K$ corrector forwards rather than
$K$ transformer blocks of a $0.6$B model. The change costs about $20$ minutes
of training and no architectural modification. It improves image quality at
every $K$, and by the largest margin where per-site residual training degrades
most.
\item \textbf{A same-step comparison with published mechanisms.} Holding the step
count and skip schedule fixed, \ourmethod{} outperforms
reproductions of TaylorSeer and Block Caching on SSIM, PSNR and ImageReward,
at an $8.4\%$ speedup cost against the former and a speedup \emph{advantage}
against the latter. Under ImageReward, both reproduced mechanisms score below
verbatim caching; learned correction is the only method that improves on it.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:diagnosis} establishes the mismatch;
Section~\ref{sec:method} replaces the objective without changing the
architecture; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} tests the replacement at a
fixed step count and at every injection granularity.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Fewer Steps Versus Cheaper Steps}
Latent diffusion \cite{rombach2022ldm} and DiT \cite{peebles2023dit} made
iterative sampling the default; PixArt-$\Sigma$ \cite{chen2024pixartsigma}
and rectified-flow transformers \cite{esser2024sd3,lipman2023flowmatching}
keep that cost structure. High-order ODE solvers
\cite{song2020ddim,lu2022dpmsolver,lu2022dpmsolverpp,zhao2023unipc} and
distillation \cite{salimans2022progressive,song2023consistency,luo2023lcm}
shorten the trajectory; caching makes each remaining step cheaper. We hold
the step count and solver fixed, which isolates caching but does not rank
the two routes (Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Training-Free Feature Caching}
DeepCache \cite{ma2024deepcache} reuses U-Net skip features. On
transformers the unit is a block residual: FORA \cite{selvaraju2024fora}
on a fixed interval; $\Delta$-DiT \cite{chen2024deltadit} with a
front/back-loaded schedule; TeaCache \cite{liu2024teacache}, ToCa
\cite{zou2024toca}, FasterCache \cite{lv2024fastercache} and AdaCache
\cite{kahatapitiya2024adacache} adapt \emph{when} to skip, then reuse
verbatim. Our correction-free baseline is uniform whole-stack step
caching. We take the schedule as given and ask what to add back.

\subsection{Learned Corrections and Training Objectives}
Learning-to-Cache \cite{ma2024l2c} learns which layers may be skipped.
LazyDiT \cite{shen2025lazydit} places a linear predictor in front of each
block and reuses from a first-order Taylor residual. Block Caching
\cite{wimbauer2024blockcaching} fits a timestep-conditioned per-channel
scale-shift --- trained, but a function of the timestep alone. TaylorSeer
\cite{liu2025taylorseer} replaces verbatim reuse with a training-free
finite-difference forecast of the cached residual. Our corrector differs
from all four in reading the \emph{current state}. That is what makes error
propagation across injection sites multiplicative
(Sec.~\ref{sec:granularity}), and it is why the training objective matters:
a state-independent correction cannot be improved by observing the
trajectory it produces.
HarmoniCa \cite{huang2025harmonica} already names an ``objective mismatch''
between aligning predicted noise and generating good images, and fixes it
with an image-error-proxy objective and step-wise denoising training. We do
not claim the mismatch itself as novel. What we add is a cheaper and more
local diagnosis --- residual error is pointwise, cache error integrates
along the latent trajectory --- and a fix that does not require an
image-space proxy: the cached rollout is already differentiable, so the
trajectory deviation can be the loss. P2 \cite{choi2022p2} and Min-SNR
\cite{hang2023minsnr} reweight the residual target; they do not replace it.
DAgger \cite{ross2011dagger} and scheduled sampling
\cite{bengio2015scheduled} correct a closed-loop data distribution. Here the
binding constraint is the objective, and the cached rollout is already cheap
to differentiate, so the resampling detour is unnecessary.

\section{The Residual Objective}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

The measurements below use PixArt-$\Sigma$-XL-2-512
\cite{chen2024pixartsigma} ($0.6$B parameters, $28$ blocks) with its shipped
DPM-Solver++ scheduler \cite{lu2022dpmsolverpp} at $20$ steps and $512^2$.
Anchors are uniform at interval $i{=}5$ ($4$ anchor steps, $16$ reuse steps).
All numbers are against the same-seed exact $20$-step output. The $28$ blocks
are grouped into $K$ contiguous cached segments, so $K$ is the number of
injection sites per reuse step: $K{=}1$ corrects the whole stack once and
$K{=}28$ corrects every block.

\subsection{Trajectory Error}
At each of the $20$ steps we measure
$\|x_{\text{cached}}-x_{\text{exact}}\|/\|x_{\text{exact}}\|$.
Deviation rises from $0.0089$ to $0.6092$, with only small dips at the four
anchors. This is the basic reason a per-step residual target is misaligned:
the corrector's action at step $t$ changes the state at $t{+}1$, while
\eqref{eq:target} is defined on the unperturbed trajectory and
\eqref{eq:resloss} never differentiates through \eqref{eq:reuse}.

\subsection{Residual Retraining}
To check that \eqref{eq:resloss} had not simply been under-optimised, we
matched data volume at $50{,}176$ slots per rung (seed multiplier $28/K$),
subsampled $48$ of $1024$ tokens per site, and doubled the step budget until
the gain fell below $0.5\%$. Residual error improved $25$--$43\%$. Image
SSIM improved by $+0.0131$ at $K{=}1$, to $+0.0055$ at $K{=}7$ ---
significant and small. Residual improvement rises with $K$ while the image
quality it buys falls.

\subsection{Oracle Correction}
An oracle that recovers a fraction $\beta$ of the true residual has residual
error $(1-\beta)^2$. Final-step trajectory error falls from $0.6102$ at
$\beta{=}0$ to $0.4384$ at $\beta{=}0.8$, and to $0$ at $\beta{=}1$. The
curve is convex: the ceiling is perfect and the middle is flat, which rules
out the correction \emph{form} as the limit and locates the problem in the
objective. Trained correctors additionally beat an oracle at their own
equivalent $\beta$ by about $1.5\times$, because a scalar oracle shrinks
error isotropically while a trained corrector learns which directions matter
to the trajectory. Residual error therefore distorts both ways: as an
objective it overstates the gain, as a capability measure it understates
the corrector.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

(b)~Residual regression supervises $C_\theta$ on the unperturbed path.
(c)~\ourmethod{} applies the discrete adjoint of the cached rollout.}
\label{fig:method}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Step Caching}
A DiT block is residual,
$R_b(h)=\mathrm{block}_b(h)-h$, so one exact block maps $h\mapsto h+R_b(h)$.
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the anchor set (here $\{0,5,10,15\}$ at interval $i{=}5$).
On $t\in\mathcal{A}$ every block runs and the residual is stored. On a reuse
step the stored residual is added instead:
\begin{equation}
h \;\mapsto\; h + R_b(h_a),\qquad
a=\max\{s\in\mathcal{A}:s\le t\}.
\label{eq:reuse}
\end{equation}
The reuse error at site $k$ is exactly
$\dR_{t,k}=R_{t,k}^{\mathrm{true}}-R_{t,k}(h_a)$, which is
\eqref{eq:target}. The residual objective regresses it on the
\emph{unperturbed} hidden states of the exact trajectory,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{res}}
\;=\;
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{A}}\sum_{k=1}^{K}
\frac{\|\dR_{t,k}-\widehat{\dR}_{t,k}\|^2}{\|\dR_{t,k}\|^2}.
\label{eq:resloss}
\end{equation}
No derivative is taken through \eqref{eq:reuse}, so \eqref{eq:resloss} cannot
see that a correction at step $t$ changes the state at $t{+}1$.

\subsection{Corrector}
The $28$ blocks are partitioned into $K$ contiguous cached segments. A
shared network $C_\theta$ is applied at each site. It reads the state drift
$\Delta h=h_t-h_a$, the cached residual $R_a$ and the anchor hidden state
$h_a$, conditioned on the diffusion timestep $t$, the reuse horizon
$\tau=t-a$ and the site index $k$. Inputs are RMS-normalised and the head is
rescaled by the target RMS $s_{\dR}$,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\dR}
\;=\;
s_{\dR}\,
C_\theta\!\left(
\frac{\Delta h}{s_{\Delta h}},\,
\frac{R_a}{s_R},\,
\frac{h_a}{s_h}
\;\middle|\; t,\tau,k
\right),
\label{eq:corrector}
\end{equation}
without which the three inputs differ in scale by up to $170\times$.
Conditioning is $c=\mathrm{MLP}([\gamma(t);\gamma(\tau);e_k])$ with Fourier
features $\gamma$ and a site embedding $e_k$. The trunk is four AdaLN
residual MLP blocks of width $512$, one $4$-head token-mixing attention
layer, and a rank-$256$ per-site linear path. The output projection is
zero-initialised, so $C_\theta{=}0$ is verbatim reuse ($11.9$M parameters at
$K{=}1$, $43.7$M at $K{=}28$). Deployment is
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathrm{out}}
\;=\;
h_t + R_a + \sigma\,\widehat{\dR},
\label{eq:deploy}
\end{equation}
with injection strength $\sigma$ selected on validation, never on test.
The Jacobian $J_k=\partial\widehat{\dR}_k/\partial h$ is nonzero because
$C_\theta$ reads $\Delta h$; that is what turns error across sites
multiplicative (Sec.~\ref{sec:granularity}).

\subsection{Pathwise Adjoint Training}
\ourmethod{} keeps \eqref{eq:corrector}--\eqref{eq:deploy} and replaces
\eqref{eq:resloss}. Let $x_1,\dots,x_T$ be the cached latents under
\eqref{eq:deploy} and $x^\star_1,\dots,x^\star_T$ the exact ones under the
same seed. The pathwise objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
\;=\;
\sum_{t\notin\mathcal{A}}
\frac{\|x_t-x^\star_t\|^2}{\|x^\star_t\|^2},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
minimised by reverse-mode differentiation through the cached rollout ---
the discrete adjoint of the sampler, restricted to reuse steps
(Fig.~\ref{fig:method}c). Supervision is free: $x^\star$ is produced by the
same forward pass that yields $C_\theta$'s input.

The adjoint is cheap for a caching-specific reason. A cached block never
runs $\mathrm{block}_b$; the runtime returns \eqref{eq:deploy}, so the
graph over one reuse step is $K$ applications of $C_\theta$, not $K$ blocks
of a $0.6$B DiT. Peak memory is $7.5$\,GB at $K{=}1$; checkpointing each
corrector call brings $K{=}14$ from over $44$\,GB to $4.2$\,GB. Gradients
are cut at anchors, where the cache is refreshed from exact values, so a
corrector call influences later intervals only through the latent.
Extending the graph across intervals improves \eqref{eq:loss} but not image
metrics; we keep the one-interval truncation.

Training uses AdamW, learning rate $10^{-5}$, gradient-norm clip $1.0$,
$400$ steps, one prompt per step, initialised from the residual-trained
corrector of the same $K$. $C_\theta$ is held in fp32 against an fp16
transformer. Cost is measured: $2.57$\,TFLOP per anchor step,
$0.041$\,TFLOP per corrector call, $2.51$\,TFLOP fixed per image. One run
is about $20$ minutes on an A40.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Every image-level number is a paired comparison over $24$ held-out prompts
$\times$ $3$ seeds ($n{=}72$), with $\sigma$ re-selected on validation for
each corrector over a common grid. We report SSIM \cite{wang2004ssim},
PSNR, LPIPS \cite{zhang2018lpips} and ImageReward \cite{xu2023imagereward},
together with $t$ statistics and the number of cases that improve. Prompts
are templated ($10$ styles $\times$ $24$ subjects $\times$ $10$ conditions);
the held-out split shares no subject with training.

\subsection{Comparison at a Fixed Step Count}

the same blocks and speedup differs only by each method's correction overhead.
We reproduce the TaylorSeer mechanism \cite{liu2025taylorseer} (Newton

and the Block Caching mechanism \cite{wimbauer2024blockcaching} (a

\subsection{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

On this backbone ImageReward saturates by $10$ solver steps while pixel
fidelity keeps improving, so an equal-compute comparison here mostly
measures solver convergence. Holding the step count fixed isolates caching.
Results are on $512^2$ PixArt-$\Sigma$ with templated prompts ($n{=}72$).
Gains are after $400$ optimiser steps without hyperparameter search, from a
residual-trained corrector, so they are what the objective change buys, not
an upper bound. High-$K$ correctors are deployed in fp32 to avoid fp16
overflow.

\section{Conclusion}

The natural objective for a learned cache corrector --- regress the residual
it should have produced --- is misaligned with what caching actually damages.
Cache error integrates along the sampling trajectory; residual error is
pointwise. A $25$--$43\%$ residual improvement buys only $+0.005$ to
$+0.013$ SSIM. Replacing the objective with \ourmethod{}, which
costs $20$ minutes of training and no architectural change, improves image
quality at every injection granularity we tested. At a fixed step count
\ourmethod{} outperforms reproductions of two published mechanisms on
three fidelity metrics and on a preference model, and is the only method in
that comparison that improves on verbatim caching under the preference model.
